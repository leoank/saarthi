## Notes
The spike relay with its hub on ghost001 (the spike built for aarch64-linux, run from that
host's store on its loopback, reached through an ssh tunnel since this Mac's own network did
not always resolve the hub's name), and the node, the client and a real `yajna serve` on
this Mac: each request crosses to ghost001 and back twice. The link, for scale (`rtt.txt`):
ping 86 ms; through ssh, 1.6 MB/s down and 0.6 MB/s up (this Mac was on a hostel's network).

| What | Direct, p50 | Relay, p50 | Same bytes |
|---|---|---|---|
| the files viewer, the index, a listing (1 to 2 KB) | 0.2 to 0.4 ms | 259 to 262 ms | yes |
| 1 MB range at 512 MB (206), 5 times | 0.9 ms | 2.2 s (3.9 s max) | yes |
| 32 MB range | 2 031 MB/s | 0.9 MB/s, first byte 260 ms | |
| change events, idle | 263 ms | 350 ms: 86 ms added | all 10 |
| change events during a 128 MB download | 263 ms | 971 ms: 709 ms added (1.7 s max) | all 5 |

- **A small request costs three round trips** (3 × 86 = 258 ms): the request to the hub, the
  hub's `OpenService` to the node, the node opening `ServiceStream` back, the request down
  it, and the answer back up and out. Sending the request's head with `OpenService`, so the
  node starts on the service while it opens the stream, would make it two.
- **An event costs one round trip** (node to hub to client), the least it can.
- **Throughput is this Mac's link**: every byte crosses its uplink (node to hub) and its
  downlink (hub to client), and 0.9 MB/s sits between the two. During a download the events
  share that uplink and wait behind it (709 ms added). HTTP/2's flow-control window is not
  the limit: hyper, under tonic, opens a stream with 1 MB (server) and 2 MB (client), about
  10 MB/s at this distance ([[RUN-20261116-ca6zg1]]).

So H1 holds over a real link: everything arrives whole and in order, events in one round
trip. The costs are round trips per request and the node's own uplink.
