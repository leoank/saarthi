## Notes
The way the owner chose on 2026-11-15 after [[RUN-20261114-rwcse4]] showed that a WebView
gets no live events through `shouldInterceptRequest`: a JavaScript bridge. Built so that
yajna's pages need not know of it: every page still goes through `shouldInterceptRequest`
(standing in for the core's stream), every HTML page gets a script at the top of its
`<head>` that replaces `window.EventSource` with one carried over a `WebMessagePort`, and
the app reads the event stream itself (`HttpURLConnection`, parsing `event:` and `data:`
lines) and posts each event to the page. Same setup as [[RUN-20261114-rwcse4]] (relay,
`yajna serve` 0.30.1, emulator, WebView 124).

- **The test page**: all ten writes arrived as events, each on its own about 1 s apart,
  241 ms after the write once the device clock's offset (402 ms behind) is taken off: the
  same as loading from a loopback port, and as yajna's own watching on the laptop.
- **The files viewer, its code unchanged**, opened at `/files/#data/log.txt`: its own
  `EventSource` went over the bridge (one stream), three appends while it was open showed
  in the open file within the second (`files-bridge.png`, the status bar's `live`), and its
  reads of the file's new bytes passed through the intercept as range requests (206). 23
  requests intercepted, no console error.

So the bridge works with nothing changed in yajna: the app replaces `EventSource` and
carries its streams; everything else is plain requests through the intercept. The core's
version replaces the spike's `HttpURLConnection` with its `ServiceRequest` stream, and needs
no port on the phone that other apps could reach.
