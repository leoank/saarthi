## Notes
cedar-policy 4.15.0 (released 2026-11-16), the spike under `spikes/16-cedar`: the schema
`saarthi.cedarschema` and six policy files (`admin`, `members`, `apps`, `monitor`, `nodes`,
`labels`, 22 policies), against a world of three people (ank an admin, bea and cy members),
two phones, two apps (`reviewer` of ank's, `builder` of bea's with a terminal grant), a
monitor app, four nodes (ghost001 shared with bea, rogue, bea-box, and `reviewer-node`
embedded in the reviewer app), their profiles, three accounts (bodygram shared with bea)
and six sessions, one labelled `keep=true`.

- H1 (schema): the 22 policies validate against the schema in strict mode, no errors, no
  warnings. Everything the hub needs was expressible: people in roles, device clients *in*
  their person so they carry its rights, apps *not* in it and held to a grant record,
  `principal in resource.shared_with` for sharing (`in` takes a set), the spawn's profile
  and account as entities in the context, labels as entity tags (`hasTag`/`getTag`).
- H2 (table): all 59 cases decide as intended, each with the policies that allowed it
  named (`run.log`). Among them: an app sees only the sessions it launched (not its owner's
  others), spawns only on granted nodes, profiles and accounts and never under a node's own
  login; a member spawns on a node shared with her only under an account shared with her;
  an embedded node gets only its app's accounts; a `keep` label forbids a kill even to the
  admin. A request outside the schema (a node streaming a session) is refused when the
  request is built, before Cedar decides.
- H4 (reload): the policy set held behind a lock, replaced without the `members` file and
  restored: the same request went Allow, Deny, Allow on the next calls.
